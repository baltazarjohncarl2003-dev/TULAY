## 2. Executive Summary & Core Marketplace Vision

**TULAY** is a proposed Philippines-focused **B2B marketplace** under *The Dream Plan*. Its purpose is to connect Filipino entrepreneurs, retailers, wholesalers, manufacturers, and other business buyers with verified suppliers through a structured online marketplace.

### The Intended Dual Experience
1. **Shopee-style product discovery:** Buyers can browse, search, discover, and compare listed products with clear specifications.
2. **Alibaba-style wholesale sourcing:** Buyers can identify suppliers, explore bulk purchasing opportunities, request quotations (RFQs), and negotiate quantity-based terms.

### Strategic Ambition
Rather than relying entirely on fragmented Facebook groups, scattered supplier contacts, or manual offline sourcing, TULAY aims to make supplier discovery and wholesale sourcing easier, more transparent, and more accessible to Philippine businesses.

### Envisioned 9-Step Core Marketplace Journey
1. A supplier registers and completes required business and profile information.
2. The supplier is reviewed and approved according to TULAY's verification rules.
3. The supplier publishes products, pricing information, minimum order quantities (MOQs), and relevant business terms.
4. A buyer searches or browses the catalog.
5. The buyer compares relevant suppliers and products.
6. The buyer requests a quotation (RFQ) or contacts a supplier.
7. Buyer and supplier communicate and negotiate quantity, pricing, delivery, and other terms.
8. The parties proceed toward an order or transaction through the workflow TULAY supports.
9. TULAY provides the platform, discovery, communication, trust, and operational mechanisms around the interaction.

> **Critical Strategic Distinction:** While the project has a clear marketplace ambition, the precise transaction model, payment flow, logistics responsibility, revenue model, supplier verification standard, and dispute-resolution system were initially left open as major strategic decisions to be resolved through validation.

## 4. The Problem TULAY Intends to Solve (Working Hypotheses)

The foundational hypothesis is that many Philippine businesses find it difficult, risky, or inefficient to discover, compare, evaluate, and contact suitable wholesale suppliers.

**Specific Pain Points Identified for Validation:**
* Supplier information is scattered across social media (Facebook groups/pages), referrals, directories, trade groups, and personal networks.
* Buyers spend excessive time searching for suppliers and confirming whether they are legitimate factories/distributors or unauthorized middlemen/scammers.
* Product listings lack consistent B2B details such as minimum order quantity (MOQ), wholesale price tiers, stock location, lead time, or business terms.
* Buyers must message dozens of suppliers individually on Messenger or Viber just to compare basic quotations.
* Small retailers and emerging entrepreneurs have limited access to established wholesale networks outside their immediate city.
* Legitimate suppliers struggle to reach qualified business buyers beyond their existing offline network.
* Buyers and suppliers lack a consistent digital process for structured inquiry, quotation comparison, and negotiation.

# PART II: INDEPENDENT AI COUNCIL RED-TEAM REVIEW & STRATEGIC OVERHAUL

## 13. The Council Debate: Five Independent Perspectives

### Framed Council Question
Should the founding team proceed with building **TULAY** (previously evaluated as BUYSON), a Philippines-focused horizontal B2B marketplace combining Shopee-style product discovery with Alibaba-style wholesale quoting and negotiation, currently prototyped on Google Apps Script and Google Sheets with zero live suppliers or products, an unvalidated category/beachhead, an undecided transaction and monetization model, and a preliminary $60\% / 25\% / 15\%$ founder equity split?

### Advisor 1: The Contrarian
TULAY is currently an empty Google Sheet masquerading as a marketplace. You are walking headfirst into the classic B2B marketplace graveyard: building a horizontal "everything store" for businesses without a single committed supplier or validated buyer. In the Philippines, B2B trade runs on two things you do not currently provide: **credit terms (*utang*/consignment)** and **relationship trust**. Sari-sari stores and small retailers do not avoid wholesale platforms because they lack a "Get Quotes" button; they buy from local wholesalers, Puregold, Suy Sing, or Facebook groups because those channels offer instant Viber/Messenger replies, cash-on-delivery, or 7-to-30-day credit terms. Even worse, if TULAY operates as a discovery and RFQ platform without handling payments, recurring workflows, or demand aggregation, you suffer **$100\%$ disintermediation** after the first introduction. Once a retailer meets a reliable packaging or FMCG supplier on TULAY, every repeat order will happen on Viber or GCash to avoid platform friction and fees. Furthermore, a $60\% / 25\% / 15\%$ equity split where the CEO holds $60\%$ before validating a single transaction risks breeding resentment in the technical lead building the platform for $25\%$.

### Advisor 2: The First Principles Thinker
Strip away the marketplace buzzwords and ask what problem actually costs Philippine SMEs money when sourcing wholesale goods. The brief conflates two completely different buyer behaviors:
1. **Replenishment buying** (sari-sari stores, neighborhood merchants buying standard FMCG inventory) requires fixed wholesale prices, low delivery fees, fast turnaround, and working capital—not a multi-step quotation and negotiation workflow.
2. **Custom or bulk sourcing** (brands, cafes, or retailers ordering custom packaging, private-label manufacturing, or unbranded bulk raw materials) requires technical specifications, sample approvals, and RFQs, where Shopee-style browsing is secondary.

By trying to blend Shopee browsing with Alibaba RFQs for everyone from sari-sari stores to manufacturers, TULAY solves neither well. At first principles, a marketplace only exists to reduce search cost, verification cost, or transaction risk. Right now, TULAY has no defined verification standard and no transaction escrow. If you do not absorb counterparty risk or aggregate buyer volume to unlock lower Minimum Order Quantities (MOQs), you are just building an online Yellow Pages directory.

### Advisor 3: The Expansionist
While a generic horizontal directory will fail, the fragmented supply chain in the Philippines hides a massive, underserved opportunity if TULAY pivots from passive listing to **demand-led RFQ matching and group-buying aggregation** in a high-margin vertical. Thousands of fast-growing Filipino online sellers (TikTok Shop, Shopee, Lazada merchants) and independent F&B operators desperately want direct-from-importer or local-manufacturer pricing in categories like custom packaging, beauty/cosmetics toll manufacturing, or food-service supplies, but they cannot meet factory MOQs alone or verify which suppliers are real factories versus middlemen. If TULAY aggregates demand or standardizes multi-factory bidding to beat middleman pricing, TULAY becomes the buyer's purchasing dept. Long-term, capturing verified RFQ and wholesale order data gives TULAY the proprietary underwriting data needed to partner with Philippine fintechs or rural banks to offer embedded B2B Buy-Now-Pay-Later (BNPL) and invoice financing.

### Advisor 4: The Outsider
Looking at how traditional merchants actually behave: first, locking in **TULAY** ("bridge") as the official name is a massive improvement over BUYSON, which sounded like an appliance store and used a foreign animal mascot. Second, ask yourself why a busy Filipino wholesaler in Valenzuela, Malabon, or Divisoria would spend hours uploading product catalogs, wholesale price tiers, and business permits onto an unknown website with zero buyers. Traditional wholesalers in the Philippines do not sit in front of desktop dashboards managing digital RFQ queues; they operate on mobile phones via Messenger, Viber, and phone calls, and they guard their wholesale price lists closely so competitors cannot undercut them publicly. Requiring suppliers to publish transparent wholesale prices upfront ignores how local B2B merchants actually negotiate.

### Advisor 5: The Executor
Stop writing code and stop debating database migrations. You already burned time switching from Next.js 16 and Supabase to Google Apps Script and Google Sheets, and your database still has **zero suppliers and zero products**. That is classic procrastination-by-architecture. You do not need OTP email verification, role-based audit logs, or automated matching algorithms to test if a buyer will source through you. On Monday morning, pick one narrow niche in Metro Manila/Central Luzon—such as commercial packaging for independent cafes, bakeries, and online brands—talk to 20 buyers, collect 10 real purchase orders they need fulfilled this week, and manually call 5 verified manufacturers to get quotes using a Google Form and Viber. If you cannot manually broker 10 wholesale deals in 30 days using spreadsheets and phone calls, no software stack on earth will save TULAY.

## 14. Council Synthesis: Agreements, Clashes & Blind Spots

### Where the Council Agrees
* **Zero Marketplace Liquidity Today:** Moving from Next.js/Supabase to Google Apps Script while maintaining $0$ live suppliers and $0$ products proves the bottleneck is commercial validation and supply-demand acquisition, not technology.
* **Horizontal Launch Will Fail:** Attempting to serve sari-sari stores, online resellers, wholesalers, and manufacturers simultaneously across 8 categories guarantees empty shelves and zero liquidity.
* **High Disintermediation Risk:** In a discovery-and-RFQ-only model, buyers and suppliers will move to Viber, Messenger, and direct bank transfers immediately after the first introduction unless TULAY controls a recurring comparison workflow, aggregates demand, or protects the transaction.
* **Suppliers Hate Public Pricing & Heavy Onboarding:** Traditional Philippine importers and manufacturers resist publishing true wholesale tiered pricing publicly (due to competitor scraping) and will not self-onboard complex catalogs without guaranteed buyer orders.

### Where the Council Clashes
* **Pure Concierge RFQ vs. Volume Aggregation:** The Executor demands stripping the model down to manual concierge RFQ matching for a single category, while the Expansionist argues simple RFQ matching is easily bypassed unless TULAY also aggregates small-buyer volume to unlock lower factory MOQs.
* **Resolution:** Start with **Manual Concierge RFQ Matching** in Days 1–60 to prove basic supply-demand completion, then test **Standardized Group-Buying / Volume Aggregation** on top of the top 5 most requested SKUs in Days 61–90.

### Critical Blind Spots Caught by the Council
1. **B2B Freight & Trucking Reality:** Unlike B2C parcels, wholesale orders involve bulky cases, pallets, and sacks. Standard parcel couriers (J&T, LBC) destroy wholesale margins outside Metro Manila, requiring Lalamove trucks, Transportify, supplier fleet delivery, or inter-island cargo forwarders. Quotes cannot be compared unless delivery/pickup terms are standardized.
2. **The Formal vs. Informal BIR Tax Divide:** Registered SME and corporate buyers require **BIR-registered Sales Invoices** (with Form 2307 creditable withholding tax handling and $12\%$ VAT clarity), whereas many informal low-cost wholesalers operate without official VAT invoicing. Mixing formal and informal suppliers in the same quote table creates apples-to-oranges pricing.

* **Alibaba / 1688 + China-to-PH Freight Forwarders:** Used by savvy e-commerce sellers for cheap unit costs, but suffers from 21–35 day sea freight lead times, customs delays, high MOQs, and lack of local BIR Sales Invoices. **TULAY's edge:** Local 3–7 day turnaround, lower MOQs, verified local factories, and official BIR invoicing.

### 15.8 Recommended Marketplace Model (Version 1)
Launch with **Model 4 (Staged Evolution)** starting strictly at **Model 2 (Structured Demand-Led Quotation Concierge)**:
* **What TULAY Does in V1:** Collects structured buyer RFQs, routes them to 3 vetted packaging manufacturers, presents a standardized side-by-side quote comparison table (unit price by MOQ tier, lead time, trucking/pickup terms, BIR invoice availability), and tracks deal closure.
* **What TULAY Does NOT Do in V1:** No open horizontal catalog of random goods; no holding buyer funds in TULAY bank accounts (avoiding BSP escrow licensing in V1); no in-house delivery fleet.

### 15.10 Trust, Verification, and Operating Model
* **Tier 1 — Registered Business Badge (Mandatory to Quote):** Verified DTI/SEC certificate, valid Mayor's Permit, **BIR Certificate of Registration (Form 2303)** confirming official invoice capability, and authorized representative government ID.
* **Tier 2 — Verified Factory / Direct Importer Badge:** Live video inspection or physical site visit by TULAY confirming warehouse/machinery location, matching utility bill, and redacted past wholesale invoices.
* **24-Hour Supplier SLA:** Suppliers ignoring 3 consecutive matched RFQs within 24 business hours are paused from receiving new leads.

### 15.13 Technology and Security Assessment
* **Suitability of Google Apps Script + Sheets:** Acceptable strictly as an **internal concierge back-office tool** for the first 60 days ($< 25$ suppliers, $< 100$ RFQs/month), but **unsuitable and insecure for open public scale**.
* **Critical Technical Risks in Current Prototype:**
  * **Concurrency Corruption:** Google Sheets lacks ACID transactions; simultaneous user writes overwrite rows unless throttled by `LockService`.
  * **Hard Daily Quotas:** Apps Script enforces a 6-minute execution limit and strict daily Gmail send caps ($100$/day consumer, $1,500$/day Workspace), which will break OTPs and notifications under load.
  * **KYC Data Exposure:** Storing business permits and government IDs in Google Drive under `Execute as: Me` risks exposing all private files if any client-facing script function lacks strict server-side authorization checks.
* **Immediate Security Fixes Required for Pilot:**
  * Never pass raw Sheet IDs or Drive Folder IDs to the client browser.
  * Restrict KYC document uploads to a locked Admin-only Drive folder and review them manually.
  * Implement server-side token validation on every `google.script.run` call.
* **Hard Migration Triggers (When Luis Resumes Next.js + Supabase/PostgreSQL):**
  1. Facilitating $\ge 20$ completed wholesale quote matches with proven repeat buyer intent.
  2. Exceeding $50$ active suppliers or $500$ SKUs (where Sheets query latency degrades UX).
  3. Before launching public self-serve catalog search or payment gateway integrations.

### 15.15 Philippine Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Map
* **Internet Transactions Act of 2023 (RA 11967):** Requires e-marketplaces to exercise due diligence in verifying merchant identities (registration, contact details) and maintain takedown/redress mechanisms to avoid secondary liability.
* **BSP Operator of Payment System (OPS) Rules:** TULAY must **not** collect buyer payments into its own bank account to hold in escrow without BSP registration or a licensed payment aggregator (e.g., PayMongo, Xendit, Dragonpay split payments).
* **Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173):** Collecting government IDs and sole proprietor business documents requires appointing a Data Protection Officer (DPO), posting a Privacy Notice, obtaining explicit consent, and securing storage.
* **IPOPHL Trademark Clearance:** Conduct an immediate search and filing for **TULAY** under **Class 35** (online wholesale/B2B marketplace services) and **Class 42** (SaaS platform).
* **BIR Tax Compliance (RR No. 16-2023):** Keeping V1 as a lead-fee/SaaS model avoids the $0.5\%$ effective creditable withholding tax administration required when e-marketplaces remit gross sales proceeds to merchants.
